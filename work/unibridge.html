UniBridge

Campus immersion visits that help high-school students from China and Taiwan choose a UK university with their eyes open: campus tours, time with current students, and programme presentations.

Proposed routes: Shanghai and Taipei to a UK campus in London. No visits have run yet.

Drag the globe or pick a city. It shows the proposed programme, not visits that have happened.

Co-founder, CTO & CPO

Three co-founders

The problem

Families choose UK degrees from the other side of the world, often without ever seeing a campus or talking to a student on the programme.

What the university saw

In my conversations with the university's admissions, marketing and outreach teams, the problem that resonated was simple: prospective students often don't know how to navigate a campus or tell programmes apart. Helping them do that before they apply could serve the university as much as the family.

My role

I lead the product and the programme: what a visit looks like hour by hour, the platform behind it, and the pricing. I also opened the institutional side by writing to the Business School's dean directly.

The approach

  • Start with the institution, not the ad campaign. Without a university willing to host, there is no product. So that conversation came first.
  • Be clear about what we are. UniBridge is a study-tour provider, not a recruitment agent. That line matters to universities, and it shapes how the programme is described and sold.
  • One pilot before scale. The plan is a single Business School pilot first, then other departments and universities.
  • Students host students. The plan is for current undergraduates to meet visiting families, because they answer the questions brochures can't.

What exists so far

  • Encouraging initial conversations with Imperial College Business School about a pilot. The format, the university's involvement and the commercial model are still under discussion.
  • A proposed one-day campus itinerary, not yet approved or run.
  • A student ambassador programme, with 16 students who have registered interest.
  • A financial model and pricing framework.

What's still uncertain

  • Whether families will pay the price the model needs. No visit has been sold yet.
  • How quickly one department's pilot turns into something the wider university wants.

Next milestone

Planned next step: introduce the programme to the university's outreach team and agree whether, and how, a first pilot visit could run.
