Huddle

A private call companion for Windows. It transcribes both sides of a call on my laptop and, when I hold a hotkey, suggests what to say next.

The problem

On a sales call or an interview, the right answer often arrives a few seconds too late. Live-call assistants exist, but most of them stream the conversation to a server. I wanted the help without sending anyone's voice off my machine.

My role

I built it alone, end to end: the audio pipeline, the overlay, the settings and history windows, and the packaging. It reuses the Electron plumbing I wrote for Clicky for Windows.

The approach

Five decisions shaped it:

  • Two channels, labelled. My microphone and the system audio (the other side of the call) are captured separately, so the transcript knows who said what.
  • Speech detection before transcription. Each channel runs through Silero VAD to cut clean speech segments, then Whisper transcribes them locally into a rolling transcript.
  • Help on demand, not a bot that never stops. Nothing is sent until I hold the hotkey. Then only the last few minutes of transcript, as text, go to Gemini, and the suggestion streams onto the overlay.
  • Private like presenter notes. The overlay window is content-protected, so it doesn't show up when I share my screen.
  • No config files for users. A settings window handles the API key, hotkey, model and how much context is kept. Session history stays on the machine and can be deleted.

The result

A tray app with a small, draggable overlay that starts listening as soon as it opens. It needs one account, a Gemini API key; everything else runs locally.
